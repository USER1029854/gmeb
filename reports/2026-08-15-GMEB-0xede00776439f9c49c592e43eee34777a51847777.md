**Target:** `0xede00776439f9c49c592e43eee34777a51847777` (BSC, chainid 56)
**Purpose:** Pre-audit reconnaissance only — this document maps the complete resolved dependency and authority graph around the target so an audit has nothing hidden to be blindsided by. It does **not** assess exploitability or make a safety determination.

## 0. TL;DR for the audit

The token contract itself (`FlapTaxTokenV3`) is a thin, fixed-supply tax token with no mint function and no owner-controlled tax setter post-launch — its own attack surface is small. **Almost all of the real power over this token's value sits one or two hops away, in shared platform infrastructure:**

1. **Ownership of the token's own tax/dividend machinery has been renounced at the token level, but not upstream.** The token's `owner()` is the zero address, so nobody can call the token's own `startMigration`/`finalizeMigration`/`transferOwnership`. But the **TaxProcessor** and **Dividend** contracts wired to it are each owned by a central `Portal` contract — and Portal's admin role is live, held by two 3-of-4 multisigs. Through Portal, an admin can still sweep any token balance sitting in the TaxProcessor or Dividend contracts (`withdrawAll` / `emergencyWithdraw`) and redirect any holder's dividend payouts (`setDividendReceiver`), with no timelock.
2. **The token's liquidity pool is not paired against a blue-chip asset — and this is a platform pattern, not a one-off.** The "quote token" on the other side of the only pool with real liquidity (and the asset dividends are paid in) is a minimal beacon-forwarder proxy running an ADGM-regulated tokenized-securities implementation (`SecuritiesToken`), branded "GameStop" / ticker "GMEB" — confusingly close to the target's own ticker. It is currently **live-mintable** by 4 addresses (1 EOA holds full admin, 3 more hold issuer rights), globally pausable, and per-address freezable by an external compliance contract. **This exact same beacon, implementation, and single-EOA beacon owner also back the quote token used by 2 of the 3 sibling tokens mapped in §5** (bBroker and MarsCoin, both priced against a "SpaceX"/SPCXB instance of the identical proxy) — so one EOA's beacon upgrade would simultaneously change the real-liquidity backing of 3 of the 4 mapped tokens at once. (The 4th, MAX, is the outlier: priced against genuine WBNB.)
3. **SwapRegistry, a wired (if not currently active) dependency of the tax flow, runs unverified bytecode behind a proxy whose upgrade key is a single EOA.**
4. **The shared TaxProcessor/Dividend template isn't one template — it's (at least) two, split exactly along the same fault line as finding #2.** GMEB and bBroker run a newer, hardened generation (overflow-safe dividend math, plus the owner-controlled `setDividendReceiver` payout-redirect). MAX and MarsCoin run an older, simpler generation that lacks both the overflow fix and the `setDividendReceiver` function entirely. Reading the older generation's fallback pricing logic against MarsCoin's actual quote token (SPCXB, priced at ~$116/unit by the *newer* generation's own hardcoded table) suggests MarsCoin's buyback/dispatch thresholds are miscalibrated by roughly two orders of magnitude — a derived, source-level finding, not empirically confirmed against live storage (§5, §6).

None of this is inherently proof of malicious intent — some of it (the Gnosis Safes, the genuine PancakeSwap infrastructure, the mostly-burned LP, the very fact that a hardened second generation of the tax/dividend template exists at all) reflects reasonable engineering. But the picture only becomes visible by walking outward from the token, and by comparing siblings against each other; nothing in the token's own verified source hints at any of it.

### 1.1 What the implementation's own code can and cannot do

Full source of `FlapTaxTokenV3.sol` was read end-to-end. Its **entire** owner-gated surface is:

- `startMigration()` — flips pool state `BondingCurve → Migrating`. In `Migrating` state, transfer restrictions and tax are both off entirely (any address, including pool addresses, can move tokens tax-free) — this is the deliberate window for the deployer to seed the AMM pool.
- `finalizeMigration()` — flips `Migrating → TaxEnforcedAntiFarmer`, and *starts the clock* on `taxExpirationTime`/`antiFarmerExpirationTime` from that moment.
- Standard `OwnableUpgradeable`: `transferOwnership`, `renounceOwnership` (already exercised — owner is now zero).

**Correction to the discovery brief:** the brief describes `startMigration`/`finalizeMigration` as moving "liquidity/state to a new destination" and flags it as "a classic rug vector if the destination is owner-controlled." Having read the actual `FlapTaxTokenV3.sol` implementation in full, **there is no destination parameter and no liquidity-moving code in either function** — they only toggle the `PackedPoolState.state` enum and timestamps. There is no `setTaxRate`/threshold setter of any kind post-initialization either — tax rates and the liquidation-threshold ceiling are fixed forever at `initialize()` time (only the threshold's *current value* self-adjusts within `[MIN_LIQ_THRESHOLD, initialLiquidationThreshold]` via the internal `_adjustLiquidationThreshold`, driven only by TaxProcessor's directional signal, not by any admin call). Since `owner()` is now the zero address, even this narrow migration-toggle surface is permanently inert. **This does not mean the token is safe** — it means the risk is not where the brief's shorthand suggested; it is entirely in the wired-to contracts below, none of which had their ownership renounced.

`maxSupply` is a `constant` (1e9 × 1e18), minted once in `initialize()` to `msg.sender`. **There is no external `mint()` function anywhere in `FlapTaxTokenV3`.** Supply is permanently fixed.

### 3.2 quoteToken — the pool's other half is not what it looks like

`0x46ceefda28dd7207059ed19b0acdc026955bb15c` is what the token's own `quoteToken()` getter (and TaxProcessor's `dividendToken()`) point to. It is verified on BscScan as `BeaconProxy`, and its own `name()`/`symbol()` are **`"GameStop"` / `"GMEB"`** — which is presumably why the discovery lane's pair label reads "utility / GMEB." This is **not** WBNB, BUSD, or USDT. Its own `totalSupply()` at snapshot time is ≈319,164.95 (18 decimals) — but see below, this number is only as meaningful as the mint authority that can change it at will.

- **Proxy pattern:** this is a compact (283-byte), hand-rolled beacon-forwarder, not OpenZeppelin's stock `BeaconProxy` — confirmed by reading the raw bytecode directly rather than trusting either the BscScan label or a single storage read in isolation. The beacon address is baked into the bytecode as a `PUSH32` immediate (`0x156d6dce9a4f6139a3406f1f021f1a4880de93a3`), and every call does a live `STATICCALL` to `beacon.implementation()` (selector `0x5c60da1b`) before delegating — so it always uses whatever the beacon currently points to, with no cached/stale implementation. The *same* address is **also** mirrored into the standard EIP-1967 beacon storage slot (`keccak256("eip1967.proxy.beacon")-1`) — independently confirmed by direct `eth_getStorageAt`, non-zero, matching — almost certainly written once by the constructor purely so block explorers/wallets that check that slot display it correctly; the runtime logic itself never reads it. Both mechanisms agree, so there is no ambiguity about the beacon's identity, just an implementation detail worth recording precisely for the audit.
  - Beacon: `UpgradeableBeacon` **`0x156d6dce9a4f6139a3406f1f021f1a4880de93a3`**.
  - `beacon.implementation()` → **`0xcfed6c4679297ea4889f8183bc057b4a86c64e46`**, verified `ContractName: SecuritiesToken`.
  - `beacon.owner()` → **`0x4333daf4481f281f3d3d2b8735ce80bc00028d0c`**, a plain EOA (0 bytecode). One private key can swap the logic behind this quote-token at will.
  - **This is not unique to GMEB.** The sibling-mapping pass (§5) found bBroker's and MarsCoin's quote token ("SpaceX"/SPCXB, `0xbe9d156892e55e7154bcd3cb0fea677f9d3103e1`) — and `SPYB` (`0x7138b48df7D98D7e3cc221BfE7192D0a178182D8`, referenced in TaxProcessor's hardcoded price table, §3.3) — run **byte-for-byte identical proxy bytecode**, on the **exact same beacon**, with the **exact same EOA owner**. Re-verified directly here: `0xbe9d1568...03e1`'s runtime bytecode is character-for-character identical to GMEB's quote token's, and its EIP-1967 beacon slot independently resolves to the same `0x156d6dce...93a3`. One EOA's beacon upgrade would rewrite the ERC-20 logic behind the real-liquidity side of 3 of the 4 mapped tokens' pools simultaneously (not MAX — see §5). How many *other* tokens beyond this mapped family sit on the same beacon was not enumerated (§6).
- **What SecuritiesToken actually is:** its own NatSpec states *"These tokens are Certificates which have been deemed to be Securities pursuant to a written determination of the Abu Dhabi Global Markets Financial Services Regulatory Authority under section 58(2)(b) of the Financial Services and Markets Regulations 2015."* It implements ERC-8056 (scaled-UI-amount / rebasing-display token), `AccessControlEnumerable`, and gates every transfer through two further external contracts:
  - `compliance` → `0x53dba7aabde774787a1f57236b235567da8e14f4` (verified, `Compliance`) — checked on every `from`/`to`/`msg.sender` in `_update`; can block specific addresses from transacting in this token.
  - `pauseManager` → `0x9fc74be63f3589485b2423984a7a0557e0cf700a` (verified, `PauseManager`) — checked on every transfer; can halt the token's transfers entirely.
  - `identifier()` on the live proxy = **`"AE000A4AVSM9"`** — an ISIN-shaped, UAE-prefixed securities identifier, consistent with the ADGM framing in the source. This reads as a real regulated-security wrapper, not a joke/parody contract, despite the "GameStop" branding.
- **Mint authority is live right now.** `mintEnabled() == true`, `burnEnabled() == true`. `mint(uint256)` is callable by anyone holding `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` or `ISSUER_ROLE`, and mints straight to the caller. Enumerated via `AccessControlEnumerable` (no log-scanning needed, so this list is exact, not inferred):
  - `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` (1 holder): **`0x45e35fe982f3869221b222abea372fa97aa7679d`** — plain EOA. Also controls role grants, `setCompliance`, `setPauseManager`, `setName`/`setSymbol`, mint/burn toggles.
  - `ISSUER_ROLE` (3 holders): `0x5948602680fe4aea08d98302337a2715b3aadad1` (an `UpgradeableProxy` → impl `0xd00eb2a87e7b699b75be975c2edae47d36cf07dd`, itself a further upgradeable layer whose own admin was not traced), `0xa42436335a41fa6c82cde224ae2b82c025027e5b` (EOA), `0xc94e485510c458860f89d23dcb8dd34bd10e6b8b` (contract, 2,145 bytes, not independently verified/named).
- **Net read for the audit:** this token — which is literally half of the only pool the target trades in, and the entire asset dividends are paid in — is a permissioned, currently-mintable, pausable, per-address-freezable security wrapper, controlled by two distinct single EOAs (one for logic-upgrade, one for operational admin) rather than a multisig. A malicious or compromised holder of either key can mint into the pool, or freeze/pause the quote side of the pool outright.

Flagged so the audit doesn't have to re-derive this from Portal's huge source — but see §6 for the narrower-role enumeration this finding does *not* cover.

### 3.6 SwapRegistry — unverified logic behind a single-EOA-controlled proxy

`0x644a8f560138418bad4edefc7c17878a3c2fbeb6` — verified `TransparentUpgradeableProxy` → implementation **`0x9a681bc1350636bbf81085207cde7485daa67a65`**.

- **The implementation itself is unverified.** BscScan returns `"Contract source code not verified"` for it — 11,045 bytes of live, non-trivial, opaque bytecode. - **Practical relevance to GMEB today: low but live.** GMEB's TaxProcessor has `dividendToken == quoteToken`, so the SwapRegistry-mediated "custom dividend token" conversion branch is never exercised right now (`converter` is unset). But `taxProcessor.setDividendToken()` is `onlyOwner` (Portal) and could redirect dividends to a third token at any time, which would immediately activate this dependency.
- **This is one of the most significant blind spots in the whole graph**: unverified logic, sitting immediately adjacent to the tax-revenue flow, upgradeable by a single key.

## 5. Shared-template check — is the brief's "flaw in one is a flaw in all" claim true at the bytecode level?

The discovery brief names 4 siblings sharing the `FlapTaxTokenV3` family: bBroker (`0xf1969f437fe3c485468fb17b0d9861c24dcd7777`), this target (`0xede00776439f9c49c592e43eee34777a51847777`), MAX (`0xe9Bc5C6A86caA44fD7b469bf3cc7c563E4F77777`), and MarsCoin (address not given — resolved here via GeckoTerminal search to **`0xfe189e97832da1573e4e4ff034f4ffc3a15c7777`**, listed there against USDT; on-chain investigation in §5.3 finds that pairing is real but economically dead dust, and the token's actual liquidity sits elsewhere. All four addresses share a vanity `...7777` suffix, consistent with a common deployer/factory tool.)

All facts below were read live on-chain (`eth_call`/`eth_getCode`/`eth_getStorageAt`, cross-checked against verified BscScan source where relevant), the same methodology as §1–§4, not inferred from the brief.

### 5.1 Headline: same token implementation everywhere, but the satellite infrastructure forks into two generations

**All 3 siblings' token contracts are EIP-1167 clones whose implementation slot decodes to exactly `0x024f18294970b5c76c0691b87f138a0317156422`** — byte-for-byte the same deployed `FlapTaxTokenV3` instance GMEB uses, confirmed via raw `eth_getCode` on each, not merely "the same verified contract name." A flaw in that implementation is a flaw in all 4 tokens' core transfer/tax logic, literally.

All four also match on: `totalSupply() == 1,000,000,000 × 1e18` exactly, `owner() == address(0)` (renounced), `v2Router()` = the same genuine PancakeRouter, `antiFarmerDuration() == 2,592,000`, `initialLiquidationThreshold() == 400,000 × 1e18`, and `state() == 2` (`TaxEnforcedAntiFarmer`) — these read as un-customized template defaults across every deployment. `buyTaxRate()`/`sellTaxRate()` do vary (GMEB 100/100 bps, bBroker 0/100, MAX 300/300, MarsCoin 300/300).

But the **taxProcessor and dividendContract clones are not all pointing at GMEB's implementations.** Direct `eth_getCode` diffs show **three mutually-distinct taxProcessor implementations** and **two distinct-but-byte-identical dividend implementations** in the mapped family:

| | GMEB | bBroker | MAX | MarsCoin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| taxProcessor impl | `0x9fe67a78...9569` | **same as GMEB** | `0x28aff281...7ae4` — different | `0xa6932beb...2b95a` — different from both |
| dividend impl | `0x666c0a0e...b031` | **same as GMEB** | `0x0116f1e4...ea65` — different | `0xde658c0a...54d8` — different *address*, byte-identical *bytecode* to MAX's |

**Every satellite clone across all four tokens — 8 clones total — is owned by the same Portal contract**, regardless of which of the two generations it runs. Centralized admin control (§3.5) is platform-wide, not GMEB-specific.

### 5.2 Two generations, diffed at the source level

Pulled and diffed verified source for all three taxProcessor implementations and both dividend implementations directly (not just names/addresses):

**"Shared" generation — GMEB + bBroker** (`taxProcessor 0x9fe67a78...9569`, `dividend 0x666c0a0e...b031`) — newer, more hardened:
- `TaxProcessorBase.sol` hardcodes a USD-price table for non-$1-pegged "stock/gold" quote tokens so buyback/dispatch thresholds size correctly instead of assuming a $1 peg: `SPYB=0x7138b48d...182D8` (~$738), **`SPCXB=0xbe9D1568...103E1`** (~$116 — exactly bBroker's and MarsCoin's quote token), `XAUT=0x21cAef8A...96A3bf` (~$4073).
- `Dividend.sol` uses Solady's `FixedPointMathLib.fullMulDiv{,Up}` for the `share × magnifiedDividendPerShare` computation, with an explicit doc comment describing a real overflow/DoS this fixes: an unbounded product can exceed `2**256` and revert `setShare` unconditionally, which "makes the token non-transferable for the affected holder" (every call site re-reverts on failure).
- `Dividend.sol` adds a deferred first-receive registration system (`deferredOrigins`/`keepers`/`refreshShares`) to avoid an out-of-gas revert on a holder's first dividend-bearing receive.
- `Dividend.sol` adds **`setDividendReceiver(holder, receiver)` / `receiverOf(holder)`** — the owner-controlled payout-redirect described in §3.4. **This function does not exist at all** in MAX/MarsCoin's dividend implementation (confirmed by grep: 0 occurrences) — so that specific redirect risk is unique to GMEB + bBroker among the four.
- Possible latent bug carried in this generation: `_processTaxTokensBody`'s `liqThresholdDirection = deferredTaxTokenBalance == 0 ? int8(0) : int8(-1)` contradicts its own doc comment two lines above, which says the zero-backlog case should return `+1`.

**"MAX/MarsCoin" generation** (`taxProcessor 0x28aff281...7ae4` / `0xa6932beb...2b95a`, `dividend 0x0116f1e4...ea65` = `0xde658c0a...54d8`, byte-identical) — older, simpler:
- No SPYB/SPCXB/XAUT price table — any quote token outside a small BNB/USD1/ASTER allowlist is assumed ≈$1/unit.
- Plain (non-`fullMulDiv`) dividend-share math; no deferred-registration mechanism; no `setDividendReceiver`.
- `_processTaxTokensBody` returns `int8(1)` on the zero-backlog case — matches its own doc comment (no code/comment mismatch here).
- **Both generations retain an identical-signature `emergencyWithdraw(token, amount, to) external onlyOwner` sweep** — that specific risk (§3.4) is common to all four tokens' dividend contracts regardless of generation.

**Derived finding (source-level, not empirically triggered):** MarsCoin's own quote token is SPCXB — priced at ~$116/unit by the *newer* generation's own hardcoded table — but MarsCoin runs the *older* generation, which has no SPCXB entry and would treat it as ≈$1/unit. Reading the older generation's fallback formula against that assumption, MarsCoin's buyback-floor and dispatch-threshold parameters would come out roughly two orders of magnitude too high in USD-notional terms. bBroker, also priced in SPCXB but running the newer/aware generation, would not have this issue. This is inferred from the two source diffs, not from reading MarsCoin's actual configured threshold values on-chain — flagged in §6 as a gap for the audit to close directly if it matters to their scope.

Deployment chronology (which generation shipped first) could not be determined — see §6.

## 6. Unresolved — the audit's explicit blind-spot list

Everything below is a real gap, not a hedge. Each entry says what it might be hiding.

1. **Deployer/creation-transaction identity and deployment chronology** for every clone in this graph (target, implementation, taxProcessor clone/implementation ×3 generations, dividend clone/implementation ×2 generations, and all 3 siblings). Etherscan's `getcontractcreation` and `account`-module actions both returned "Free API access is not supported for this chain" on the supplied API key's tier. `eth_getLogs` was tried at multiple range sizes — unbounded, 40,000-block-bounded, and (by the sibling-mapping pass) as narrow as a 5-block window — and was rejected every time (`{"code":-32005,"message":"limit exceeded"}`) regardless of range on both public RPC endpoints used (`bsc-dataseed.binance.org`, `bsc-dataseed1.defibit.io`). A binary-search-by-`eth_getCode` approach to find deployment blocks was also tried and abandoned: both endpoints are **non-archive nodes** — `eth_getCode` returns empty for *any* contract, including BEP-20 USDT (live since 2020), at roughly 85 blocks or more behind the current tip, so the search just finds the node's state-pruning boundary, not the real deployment block. *Could hide:* whether a single factory/EOA deployed all 4 siblings back-to-back (strongly suggested by the shared owner=Portal pattern on every taxProcessor/dividend clone, but not independently confirmed by a creation-tx trace), which of the two taxProcessor/dividend generations shipped first, or a deployer identity worth reputational cross-referencing. Closing this gap needs either a paid Etherscan tier or archive-node RPC access.
2. **≈2,110 LP tokens (≈0.69% of mainPool's LP supply)** are not held by `0xdEaD`, `address(0)`, taxProcessor, Portal, or the token contract. Holder not identified. *Could hide:* a small pool of liquidity that is technically pullable by whoever holds it (though at ~0.69% of the pool, the practical impact of pulling it alone is limited).
3. **Top-holder concentration of the ~97% of GMEB supply outside known infrastructure addresses.** No token-holder-list indexer was reachable on this API tier; enumerating would require full Transfer-log history, which hit the same RPC range limits as #1. *Could hide:* a small number of wallets (e.g. team/deployer-linked) holding a large fraction of circulating supply.
4. **metaURI contents** (IPFS CID `bafkreifbawz...`) — gateway fetch returned 403. *Could hide or reveal:* claimed project branding, team info, or external links; entirely unverified either way.
5. **Portal's narrower AccessControl roles** — `AUDITOR_ROLE`, `GUARDIAN_ROLE`, `MANAGE_ROLE`, `TOKEN_FLAP_FEE_SETTER_ROLE`, `MODERATOR_ROLE` — membership was not enumerated. Only `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` was confirmed (via direct `hasRole()` probes against the two Safes already identified from other angles). *Could hide:* additional addresses — possibly EOAs, possibly outside the two identified Safes entirely — with meaningful operational control over Portal (e.g. `TOKEN_FLAP_FEE_SETTER_ROLE` sounds directly relevant to this token's fee configuration).
